Free ATS CV Checkers: What They Can and Can't Tell You

Free checkers are useful for spotting formatting problems. Here is how to read their scores and when you need more than a scan.

What free checkers do well

They flag missing sections, unreadable formatting and obvious keyword gaps against a pasted job description. That alone can save you from avoidable mistakes.

Why the score is not the full story

Scores come from each tool's own rules, not from the employer's system. A high score does not mean a recruiter will shortlist you, and a low score does not always mean your CV is bad.

What they cannot fix

A checker cannot tell which achievements matter most, how to describe a career change, or how to tailor your story to a specific employer. That needs judgement.

How to use them well

Run a check, fix formatting issues, then read your CV as a recruiter would. Ask: is my target role obvious in ten seconds? Are my results clear?

When to get help

If you have applied widely with few responses, the issue is usually content and targeting. That is where a professional rewrite helps most.

Frequently asked questions

Are free ATS checkers accurate?

They are useful for spotting obvious issues such as missing headings, unreadable formatting or few matching terms. Their scores are estimates based on their own rules, not the settings of the employer you are applying to, so treat the number as a hint rather than a verdict.

What score should I aim for?

Instead of chasing a number, check that the tool can read your name, contact details, job titles and dates correctly, and that important skills from the job advert appear in your CV where they are true. A readable, relevant CV matters more than a perfect score.

Should I pay for an ATS checker?

Usually not at first. Try a couple of free tools, fix what they flag, and then ask a person to review the story your CV tells. Software can check structure; it cannot judge whether your achievements are persuasive for the role.
