The STAR Method for Interview Answers

A simple structure for answering behavioural interview questions clearly.

What STAR stands for

Situation, Task, Action, Result. It keeps your answers focused and easy to follow.

Situation and task

Set the scene in one or two sentences: where you were and what needed to happen.

Action

Spend most of your answer here. Explain what you did, not what the team did.

Result

Share the outcome and, if possible, what you learned.

Practise with real examples

Prepare five or six stories from your experience that cover teamwork, conflict, leadership and problem-solving. Mock interviews help you refine them.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a STAR answer be?

Aim for about one and a half to two minutes. Spend little time on the situation and task, most of it on your actions, and finish with a clear result. Practise out loud so your answers stay focused.

What if my result was not a success?

You can still use STAR. Explain what happened, what you learned and what you would do differently. Interviewers value honesty and reflection, especially for questions about mistakes or challenges.

How many STAR stories should I prepare?

Prepare six to eight stories that cover common themes: teamwork, conflict, leadership, a challenge, a mistake, achieving a goal and handling pressure. One good story can often answer several different questions.
