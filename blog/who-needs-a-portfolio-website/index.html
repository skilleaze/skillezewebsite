Who Needs a Personal Portfolio Website?

When a portfolio helps your job search, and what it should include.

Roles where it helps most

Designers, developers, marketers, writers, architects and consultants benefit most because their work can be shown, not just described.

It helps freshers too

Projects, case studies and certifications look more credible on a portfolio than in a CV bullet.

What to include

A short introduction, skills, experience, three to six strong projects and a clear way to contact you.

Keep it fast and mobile-friendly

Recruiters often open links on their phones. Simple, fast pages work best.

Use it everywhere

Add the link to your CV, LinkedIn, job portal profiles and email signature.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a portfolio if I am not a designer?

Not always, but it helps anyone whose work can be shown: developers, marketers, writers, analysts, architects and educators. A simple site with your story, a few case studies and a contact link can set you apart from CV-only applicants.

Should I buy my own domain name?

A personal domain such as yourname.com looks professional and is easy to share on your CV and LinkedIn. It is not essential at first, but it makes your portfolio feel permanent and is usually inexpensive.

How many projects should I show?

Three to six strong, relevant projects are better than a long list. For each one, explain the problem, your role, what you did and the result, with visuals or links where you can share them.
